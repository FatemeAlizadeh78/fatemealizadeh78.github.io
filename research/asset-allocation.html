How should uncertainty change an allocation?

A rolling comparison of constrained portfolio optimization, resampling, and Bayesian views across 20 multi-asset ETFs.

Financial Markets Analytics2026Explore the source code ↗

The question

How do sampling uncertainty and momentum-based investor views change portfolio weights, realized risk, and out-of-sample returns?

Experimental design

  • Converted FactSet gross total-return levels into monthly returns and estimated each allocation using only the preceding 60 months.
  • Used SciPy SLSQP for long-only, fully invested portfolios with a 20% single-ETF cap. Compared equal-weight and 60/40 baskets, Markowitz minimum variance, and maximum Sharpe portfolios.
  • Implemented Michaud resampled minimum variance with 100 Gaussian histories per rolling window.
  • Built Black–Litterman posterior means and covariance from reverse-optimized priors, absolute/relative trailing-12-month momentum views, confidence-weighted Omega, and shrinkage. Inspected weight-cap and tau sensitivity.
  • Evaluated 160 monthly out-of-sample allocations from February 2013 through May 2026, with arithmetic annual return, volatility, zero-risk-free-rate Sharpe, maximum drawdown, and target-weight changes.

What the experiment does not establish

  • The ETF universe was selected retrospectively; survivorship and research selection effects can remain.
  • No additional untouched evaluation period or statistical significance claim is supplied.
  • FactSet inputs are licensed and excluded from Git. Synthetic demonstration data are clearly separate from the empirical experiment.
  • This 2026 FMA study is distinct from the July 2025 S&P 500 / K-means portfolio project in the Business and Marketing lab.
